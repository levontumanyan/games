# Workout Science & Programming Framework

This document outlines the evidence-based exercise science principles, biomechanical guardrails, and master weekly architecture for designing and adapting Levon's workout routines.

Any AI agent, subagent, or coach authoring or modifying routines in this app must strictly adhere to these scientific standards.

# Athlete Profile & Hypertrophy Targets

## Primary Growth Targets
* **Quads & Thighs**: Focus on high-threshold motor unit recruitment via deep knee flexion and mechanical tension (e.g., Bulgarian split squats, sissy squats, heel-elevated squats).
* **Shoulders (Deltoids)**: Heavy emphasis on lateral and rear delts to build shoulder width and balance the shoulder girdle. Minimize anterior delt volume, which is already saturated from pushups and Muay Thai striking.
* **Chest**: Full-range sternal and clavicular loading through deep stretch pushups (deficit/rings) or presses.
* **Biceps & Forearms**: Direct elbow flexion (curls, chin-ups) paired with wrist flexor/extensor and grip work (hangs, towel grips, reverse curls).

## Disciplines & Activities
* **Hypertrophy & Strength**: Zero-equipment calisthenics using bodyweight and household furniture (beds, chairs, doorframes, towels), park pull-up bars, and occasional commercial gym access.
* **Combat Sports**: Muay Thai striking, checks, knees, teeps, and combination flow.
* **Aquatic Conditioning**: Swimming for non-impact aerobic capacity, lung volume, and spinal decompression.
* **Mobility**: Active range of motion and joint capsule health.

## Training Cadence
* **Continuous Progression**: Maintain consistent, progressive overload week-over-week across the master weekly schedule. Volume and intensity are adjusted manually when needed based on feel.

# Scientific Hypertrophy Principles

## 1. Mechanical Tension & Proximity to Failure (RIR)
* **Effective Reps Model**: Muscle growth occurs predominantly during the final 4–5 reps before failure, where high-threshold motor units are involuntarily recruited.
* **Target Intensity**: Sets must be executed at **1 to 3 Reps in Reserve (RIR)** (RPE 7.5–9). Junk volume (sets ending 5+ reps away from failure) adds systemic fatigue with minimal hypertrophic stimulus.
* **Rep Range Spectrum**: Any rep range between **6 and 30 reps** induces comparable hypertrophy if taken close to failure. When working at home without heavy weights, use biomechanical leverage (unilateral limbs, paused eccentrics, deficits, 1.5-reps) to keep working sets within this 6–30 rep threshold.

## 2. Weekly Set Volume & Session Caps
* **Weekly Volume per Muscle Group**: **10 to 18 hard working sets per week** distributed across 2 sessions (2x/week frequency to maximize the 24–48 hour Muscle Protein Synthesis window).
* **Session Volume Cap**: **4 to 6 working sets per target muscle per workout**. Beyond 6–8 sets in a single session, muscle protein synthesis plateaus and additional volume becomes "junk volume" that impairs recovery.
* **Session Time Limit**: Single workouts should strictly adhere to a **45 to 50 minute ceiling** to avoid excessive cortisol spikes and maintain high neuromuscular output.

## 3. The Science of Rest Intervals
Rest periods must balance mechanical tension with flow, session pacing, and time efficiency. Long rests (2–3 minutes) cause excessive downtime and cooling off during bodyweight training.
* **Challenging Compounds & Unilateral Work** (Bulgarian split squats, deficit pushups, pike pushups): **60 to 90 seconds** (never exceed 90 seconds). This restores ~85–90% of cellular phosphocreatine without dragging out the workout.
* **Accessory & Isolation Work** (Towel curls, calf raises, forearm holds, prone raises): **45 to 60 seconds**.
* **Combat Drill Rounds**: **30 to 60 seconds** rest between active rounds.
* **Midpoint Recovery Break**: **90 to 120 seconds** between major demanding blocks (e.g. before the final pushup burnout) to reset the nervous system and clear local metabolite buildup.
* **Transition into Cooldown / Stretching**: **10 seconds strictly**—no long breaks needed between the final working set and the start of cooldown stretches.
* **Mobility & Stretch Transitions**: **5 seconds strictly** between individual stretches or mobility poses—just enough time to reposition the body without dead time or losing warmth.
* **Antagonist / Alternating Flow**: When possible, alternate sides or opposing movements with 45–60s intervals to maintain an engaging, 35–45 minute flow.

# Biomechanical Guardrails & Injury Prevention

## 1. The Anterior Capsule Trap (Shoulder Protection)
* **The Risk**: Pushups, pike pushups, Muay Thai punches/elbows, and freestyle swimming all place repetitive internal rotation and anterior shear on the glenohumeral joint. Unbalanced, this leads to subacromial impingement and rotator cuff tendinopathy.
* **The Rule**: Maintain a strict **1:1 Push-to-Pull ratio**. Every pressing workout must include equal volume of **horizontal pulling** (inverted rows, gymnastics ring rows, door towel rows) or external rotation / rear delt work (face pulls, band pull-aparts).

## 2. Decoupling Heavy Quad Loading from Ballistic Kicking
* **The Risk**: Deep split squats and quad work exhaust stabilizing structures (*gluteus medius*, *vastus medialis oblique*, and ankle rotators). Throwing high-velocity roundhouses with fatigued stabilizers prevents a full pivot on the ball of the foot, diverting explosive torque directly into the **knee (ACL/meniscus)** and lumbar spine. Similarly, teeping with exhausted hip flexors triggers lumbar hyperextension.
* **The Rule**: Never program ballistic kicking drills immediately following heavy leg sets. Place intense striking on dedicated combat days or after a non-interfering upper body session.

## 3. Swimming Integration (Zero Eccentric Stress)
* **The Benefit**: Swimming provides aerobic conditioning, mitochondrial development, and cardiovascular health with zero eccentric loading and zero ground-reaction force, preserving knee and ankle joints.
* **Programming**: Use swimming as an active flush / aerobic session. Keep it separated from upper-body pressing days to prevent pre-exhausting the lats, deltoids, and triceps.

## 4. Loaded Mobility vs. Static Yoga
* **Modern Science Consensus**: Full-range resistance training with controlled eccentric loading (e.g., full-depth split squats, deep deficit pushups, Romanian deadlifts) induces *sarcomerogenesis in series* (elongation of muscle fascicles), creating functional mobility equal or superior to passive static stretching.
* **Efficiency**: A standalone 45–60 minute static yoga day is an inefficient use of time and does not clear muscle damage or DOMS.
* **The Rule**: Integrate mobility as **5 to 8 minutes of dynamic preparation** before lifting and **10 to 15 minutes of deep hip/thoracic capsule openers** (`90/90 Hip Flow`, `Pigeon Pose`, `Frog Stretch`) immediately following swimming or as an evening unwind.

# Master Weekly Architecture

This 7-day schedule balances natural hypertrophy, combat skills, swimming, and joint preservation while fitting a stable base:

## Monday: Upper Body Hypertrophy (Chest, Lateral Delts, Biceps, Grip)
* **Focus**: Sternal/clavicular chest pressing, strict lateral raises, elbow flexion, forearm grip.
* **Duration**: ~45–50 minutes.

## Tuesday: Lower Body Hypertrophy (Quads, Thighs, Adductors, Calves)
* **Focus**: Unilateral quad mechanical tension (Bulgarian split squats), knee extension (sissy squats / elevated squats), adductors, calves.
* **Guardrail**: No ballistic kicking drills. Protect the knees.
* **Duration**: ~40–45 minutes.

## Wednesday: Muay Thai Skill & High-Velocity Combat Flow
* **Focus**: Fresh central nervous system. Shadowboxing combos, pad/bag work, teep power, rotational core.
* **Duration**: ~40–45 minutes.

## Thursday: Upper Body Hypertrophy (Delts Focus, Horizontal Pull, Biceps, Forearms)
* **Focus**: Vertical pushing (pike pushup progression), horizontal pulling (inverted rows / face pulls for shoulder balance), bicep curls, wrist rolls.
* **Duration**: ~45–50 minutes.

## Friday: Lower Body Hypertrophy + Light Technical Shadow Flow
* **Focus**: Walking lunges or reverse deficit lunges, hamstring/glute hinge, followed by light, non-fatiguing Muay Thai flow.
* **Duration**: ~40–45 minutes.

## Saturday: Swimming & Parasympathetic Deep Mobility Flush
* **Focus**: 25–30 minutes of aerobic swimming (freestyle, breaststroke, backstroke) followed by 15 minutes of deep hip capsule mobility (`90/90`, `Pigeon`, `Couch Stretch`).
* **Duration**: ~40–45 minutes.

## Sunday: Complete Rest & Growth Day
* **Focus**: Zero structured training, sleep restoration, and caloric/protein replenishment.

# Contextual Equipment Substitution Rules

When tailoring any session, adapt dynamically to the environment:

## Home & Zero-Equipment Mode
* **Quads & Thighs**: Bulgarian split squats (rear foot on bed/chair/luggage), bodyweight sissy squats (holding doorframe for balance), skater squats, tempo 1.5-rep squats.
* **Chest & Triceps**: Deficit pushups on books/blocks/handles for deep chest stretch, feet-elevated decline pushups, diamond pushups, chair dips.
* **Pull & Biceps**: Doorframe rows, towel-around-door rows, under-table inverted rows, chin-ups/pull-ups on outdoor park bars.
* **Shoulders**: Feet-elevated pike pushups, prone floor Y-T-W raises, wall handstand holds.
* **Forearms**: Towel-door grip holds, reverse-grip doorframe rows, fingertip pushup holds, outdoor bar dead hangs.

## Gym Mode
* **Quads**: Barbell/safety-bar squats, leg press (feet low on platform for quad emphasis), leg extensions.
* **Chest**: Incline dumbbell press, weighted dips, cable flyes.
* **Pull & Biceps**: Lat pulldowns, chest-supported dumbbell rows, incline dumbbell curls, cable hammer curls.
* **Shoulders**: Overhead press, cable lateral raises (behind-the-back for continuous tension), face pulls.
* **Forearms**: Barbell wrist curls, farmer carries, heavy dumbbell holds.
